Where to Get Resources (Data, Partnerships, and Funding)
To build this benchmark during your hackathon and beyond, you will need to source localized data and connect with existing institutions:
* For Linguistic Data & Translation: Tap into the existing networks of independent African fact-checking organizations. Organizations like Africa Check actively process inquiries and publish verified data across local languages including Zulu, Afrikaans, Swahili, Hausa, and Yoruba. They already build data-driven tools and could be invaluable resources for localized text.

* For Legal & Public Sector Data: The African Union's Continental AI Strategy pushes heavily for government-led digital datasets to be made available through national open data portals. You should look for South African open-source legal repositories (such as SAFLII) or government portals to mine court opinions and legal texts.

* For Testing Environments: The AU strategy encourages the creation of "regulatory sandboxes" for AI innovation. You can position your benchmark as a tool to be used within these sandboxes, providing a safe environment to test AI solutions for bias before they are deployed in the real world.
